<div class="mg-intro">

The factory: a ticket comes in from an issue tracker, a support widget or an alert. An agent takes it, writes the fix and opens a pull request. In the most aggressive setups it merges and deploys **with no human in between.**

Companies are building toward that right now. The question is not whether it works on a demo. It is what makes such a line safe to run every day:

- a harness that **denies** rather than suggests,
- evals you can trust **more than your own eye**,
- a **cost per task**, not per month,
- an automated reviewer that says **what blocks prod**,
- and a record of all of it.

This course builds those parts one module at a time. You pass a module by making its tests go green. Lessons carry the date their sources were checked and the versions they ran against. Where a claim needs a model, the course ran it once and **committed the numbers**.
